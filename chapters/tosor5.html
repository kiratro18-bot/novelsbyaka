The Days the Sky Forgot to Cry

☔

The rain didn't return for weeks.
Blue skies stretched endlessly, cloudless and loud.
The kind of days people celebrated—warm, golden, buzzing with energy.
Rika hated them.

It's not that she missed the rain exactly.
It's that, without it, everything else got louder.
She still laughed with her friends.
Still played the part.
But somewhere in between jokes and hallway noise, she caught herself checking the stairwell window.
Wondering if he was already waiting.

He was.
Every time.
But something was different.

Minato smiled when he saw her.
Said all the right things.
Walked her home.
But he seemed distant—like part of him had folded inwards.

· · ·

It started the day she told him:
"My mom's moving. A transfer. Not far. But enough that I won't be at this school anymore."

She said it casually.
Lightly.
Like it didn't matter.

Minato didn't react right away.
Didn't tell her what it felt like to finally be seen by someone—only to watch them step into the distance again.

· · ·

The last Friday of the term, they met behind the school again.
This time, no umbrella.
No rain.
Just that dry breeze of early summer.

Rika handed him something—a folded note, sealed with a sticker shaped like a raindrop.

Minato didn't open it.

A confession without words
She looked at him and smiled—not like she used to.
Not with her armor.
But softly.
Honestly.
Like it was goodbye.

"I'm scared," she admitted.
"Not of leaving. But of forgetting how this felt."

Minato finally reached into his bag.
He pulled out the same paper crane—hers—now worn, edges a little bent.
He placed it in her hands.

"I won't forget," he said.

They stood there, silent.
No promises made.
No confessions.
Just two people holding the memory of a storm they survived together.

And as she walked away, Rika whispered,
"Maybe in the next rain…I'll see you again."

When the Rain Returns Again
One year later
· · ·

A full year later.
The sky broke open in late June—a sudden downpour that caught everyone off guard.
Students rushed to cover.
Cars slowed.
The smell of wet pavement filled the air.

Minato stood under the old cherry tree—the same one where stories always seemed to begin.
He hadn't been there in months.
Not since graduation.
Not since she left.

He didn't expect anything that day.
Didn't bring an umbrella.
But he did carry something in his pocket—a folded note, the one Rika gave him before she left.
He never opened it.
Until now.

Minato slowly peeled the sticker, careful not to tear the paper.
Inside, it said:

Her words
"You were never just the boy with the umbrella.
You were the one who stayed when it stopped raining.

If you ever need someone to walk beside you again…
I'll still forget my umbrella."

He smiled.
The kind that hurt.
The kind that healed.

And then…
A voice behind him.

"…You didn't bring an umbrella again?"

He turned.
There she was.
Soaked.
Windblown.
Rika.
No hoodie.
No act.
Just her.

He didn't speak.
Just walked toward her, pulled the umbrella from his bag—a new one, blue, just like before—and opened it above them.

She blinked.
"You actually carry one now?"

He nodded.
"For you," he said softly.
"Always."

They didn't kiss.
Didn't run into each other's arms.
They just stood under the rain, side by side, hands brushing gently.
And this time, no one reached first.
They met halfway.

Another pact
Some people think love is about grand confessions.
Others think it's about fate, or flowers, or timing.

But Rika and Minato knew the truth.
Love was an umbrella quietly opened.
A crane folded by uncertain hands.
A silence shared when the world turned loud again.

And when the rain came back, they didn't hide from it.
They walked through it together.
Same city.
Same tree.
A new storm survived.
