The Final Letters

Saturday, 4 PM.
Nao arrived early.
Not because she was nervous. (She was.)
Not because she thought he might already be there. (She hoped.)
But because some things — some people — you don't keep waiting.

The sakura tree was in full bloom now.
The wind stirred the petals like soft, weightless memories.

The bench looked smaller today. More fragile.

Like it remembered all the things left behind on its surface.

She sat down.

No letter this time.
No notebook.
Just her.

A girl who had once run from something.
Now waiting for someone.

Five minutes passed.

Then ten.

And just when doubt began to whisper in her ear, she heard the soft scrape of shoes on gravel.

She didn't turn.

Not yet.

She felt him sit beside her, carefully. A soft space still between them. Not hesitant. Just respectful.

They sat like that for a while.
Both of them breathing like the air had changed.

Finally, he spoke.

"I saw your note."

His voice was familiar now. She didn't flinch at it.

Didn't run from it.

Nao looked at him. Really looked.

Haruki.
Older than she imagined, but not by much.
Eyes kind.
Voice steady.

The same boy who'd written about a rose and a prince and the kind of heartbreak that doesn't leave bruises — just shapes the way you love.

She nodded. "And?"

Haruki smiled softly.
"You're her."

"Who?" she asked, even though she already knew.

"The girl who reads Banana Yoshimoto under a blooming tree and writes letters to strangers who don't feel like strangers."

She exhaled — half a laugh, half a release.

"You're him," she said. "The boy who waited even when he didn't have to."

He looked down. "Maybe I was waiting for something else. Not her. Not anymore."

Nao didn't say anything. She just held the silence with him. It didn't feel empty.

Then, slowly, Haruki reached into his coat pocket.

Not a letter.

Not a note.

Just a small, tattered bookmark — the kind you get at used bookstores.

He placed it on the bench between them.

📖 The Bookmark

"What is essential is invisible to the eye."
– The Little Prince

She stared at it.

Then at him.

Then, for the first time, she reached across that soft space between them and held his hand.

It was warm. Real.
No paper between them.
No ink.

Just skin and breath and something quietly blooming.

✿ ✿ ✿

Spring came early that year.

Students filled the courtyard again — snapping photos, laughing too loud, chasing petals like they meant something.

Nao and Haruki sat on the old bench beneath the sakura tree.

No floorboard was lifted.
No notes were passed.
The book with no cover still rested in its hiding place, untouched now for a week.

They didn't need it anymore.

"I used to think letters were safer," Nao said, breaking the comfortable silence.

Haruki looked at her. "They are."

She nodded. "But I don't want safe anymore."

He didn't respond at first. Just took a slow sip from the thermos they shared now, his shoulder brushing lightly against hers.

Then he said, "Me neither."

They watched as a group of first-years passed by, giggling and pointing at the sakura blossoms.

"I wonder how many people have left things here," she said, glancing down at the bench.

"Too many," Haruki murmured. "And maybe not enough."

Nao smiled.

The kind that belonged to her now. The kind that no longer needed hiding behind paper and ink.

After a moment, she reached into her bag and pulled out a small, sealed envelope.

"No name?" Haruki asked.

"No," she said. "It's not for anyone. Not anymore."

She stood, walked to the tree, and tucked it gently beneath the floorboard — next to the book with no cover.

"What does it say?" he asked.

Nao looked at him, eyes soft. "Everything I don't need to say out loud anymore."

And with that, she returned to his side.

🌸 The Bloom

The wind stirred, and petals fell.
Some caught in their hair. Some scattered across the bench.
Some drifted down and disappeared between the pages of forgotten letters.

But none of it felt sad.
Not anymore.

Because the tree was still blooming.
And they were still here.

No more waiting.
No more pretending.

Just the sound of spring and the things they chose to hold — and let go — together.
