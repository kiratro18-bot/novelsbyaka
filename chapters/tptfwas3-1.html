Regret

What we leave behind .

POV: Sora Memory Regret By AKA

The university library was too quiet.

Pages turned somewhere behind me. A chair scraped softly across the floor. Outside the tall windows, the sky hung dull and pale, like it hadn't decided what it wanted to be yet.

I stared at the open book in front of me without reading a single word.

Because this all started back then.

💭 Memory

"I really like you, Sora."

Ren's voice came back to me as clearly as if he were standing there again—soft, shaking, honest in a way that scared me. Rain had poured down that day, soaking his hair, his clothes, the space between us. I remember how my heart had slammed against my ribs, how badly I wanted to say the words back.

I did like him.

But I was leaving.

Transfer papers sat heavy in my bag, unreadable but unavoidable. One more week. One more week before I disappeared from his life completely.

I was scared.

Scared that if I said my feelings were the same, I wouldn't be able to leave. Scared that staying would hurt more than running.

So instead of explaining.

Instead of answering properly.

…I ran.

🏫
A new school. A new start. Or so everyone said.

When I moved away, everything was supposed to feel new.

A new high school. New classrooms. New faces. People said it would be a fresh start.

It wasn't.

The school was fine. The teachers were fine. But I didn't make friends. Or maybe I just didn't let myself. Hallways passed by in gray blurs, whispers I pretended not to hear, lunch breaks spent alone.

Then one day, I noticed it.

A sakura tree.

Blooming in November.

It stood there like it didn't care about seasons or rules—soft pink petals clinging stubbornly to bare branches. I remember stopping in my tracks, staring at it, wondering how something like that could exist.

Curiosity pulled me closer.

But I wasn't alone.

A boy sat beneath it, hands in his pockets, staring at nothing in particular. He was in my class. I recognized him immediately. For a second, I thought about saying hi.

I didn't.

I told myself the seat was probably taken. A stupid excuse. I turned away and walked off, pretending my chest didn't feel tight.

Funny thing is… we still became friends.

Another day, another meeting beneath that same tree. We talked. About small things at first. Then bigger ones. Time slipped by quietly, the way it always does when you're not paying attention.

And then—on another rainy evening, in a school corridor that smelled faintly of wet concrete—he confessed.

Not loud. Not dramatic. Just honest.

I remember talking too much after that. Saying things I didn't fully understand yet. Telling him I might hurt him. Telling him I was happy I'd found someone like him.

For the first time in a long while, I wanted to stay.

But I didn't.

I transferred again.

🌸 The Weight of It

I wanted to stay.
The words felt heavier now than they ever had back then.

I closed my eyes in the library, my fingers tightening around the edge of the desk.

"Wow…" I murmured under my breath, barely louder than the rustling pages around me.

"What a memory."

The library stayed quiet.

And so did my regret.

� Sora

Some things don't hurt less with time.
They just hurt differently.

Quieter. Deeper.
Like rain that never quite stops.

The Boy Beneath the Tree
Where old memories find new shapes.
🌸 The Petal That Falls With a Smile Vol. 3
POV: Haruki New Beginnings Sakura

The university campus felt too big.

Buildings stretched endlessly in every direction, students flooding the open paths like rivers that never stopped moving. Laughter, voices, footsteps—everything overlapped until it became noise.

"The university is huge, Haruki!" Noa said, spinning once in place. "Look at that building. And that one. And—everything!"

She was glowing with excitement, the kind that came naturally to her. Being in the same university as Haruki meant something special to her, and she wasn't trying to hide it. She walked closer, clearly hoping they'd spend more time together.

Haruki nodded absentmindedly.

"Yeah… it is," he replied, though his eyes weren't really on the campus.

He was somewhere else. As usual.

"No seriously," Noa continued, tugging lightly at his sleeve. "At least pretend you're here."

Before Haruki could answer, Noa suddenly stopped.

"Oh! Look!" she said, pointing ahead. "Haruki, a sakura tree!"

🌸
He froze. The tree did something else entirely.

The tree stood quietly near the edge of the open courtyard—petals full, branches strong, blooming exactly when it was supposed to. Sakura blooming in the right season was rare enough to catch anyone's attention.

But for Haruki, it did something else entirely.

"This one's blooming properly," Noa said with a small laugh. "Ahh… brings back memories, right?"

Haruki swallowed.

"Yeah," he said quietly. "A lot of them."

Beneath the tree, someone was sitting.

A boy—alone, shoulders slightly slouched, eyes distant. He looked like he was carrying winter inside him despite the season.

Noa noticed him too and lowered her voice.

"Let's not bother him."

"Yeah," Haruki agreed.

But even as they turned to walk away, Haruki glanced back at the tree… and the boy sitting beneath it.

And then—

A memory surfaced, sudden and sharp.

💭 Memory

"I'm a student council delivery boy."

Haruki's chest tightened.

"No—" he muttered under his breath.

"Haruki!"

Noa waved her hand in front of his face. "Haruki—HARUKI!"

He blinked. "Oh—yeah. Sorry. Lost in thought."

She sighed, half-amused, half-annoyed. "Figures. Anyway, I've got my psychology class. I'll see you later, okay?"

"Yeah. See you."

Noa walked off, disappearing into the crowd.

Haruki stayed where he was.

Why now? he wondered. Why this memory… and in this place of all places?

A familiar name slipped into his thoughts without permission.

Sora.

She was still there. Somewhere in his mind, quiet but persistent.

Almost without realizing it, Haruki turned back and walked toward the sakura tree. He sat down nearby, the shade falling gently over him.

The boy beneath the tree looked up.

"Hello," the boy said.

Haruki hesitated for a second before replying.

"Hello."

"My name's Ren," the boy continued, a faint smile forming. "Isn't this tree beautiful?"

Haruki looked up at the blossoms, then back at him.

"Nice to meet you, Ren," he said. "Yeah… it really is."

The petals stirred softly in the breeze.

And somewhere between falling blossoms and unfinished memories, something began to shift.

🌸 Haruki

Some names stay with you.
Not because you hold onto them.

But because they never quite let go.
