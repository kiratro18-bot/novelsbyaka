toll the bell

A tale of Veyr

The Bell That Rang
for the Dead

In Veyr, the dead were burned. Never buried.

In the kingdom of Veyr, the dead were not buried.

They were burned.

Everyone knew why.

A dead body left underground for more than three days would eventually stand up.

Nobody knew what happened after that.

Nobody wanted to.

So every town had a bell.

When someone died, the bell rang once.

When the body was taken to the furnace, it rang twice.

And if it ever rang three times...

Everyone ran.

Elias had been the Bellkeeper of Veyr for six years.

He hated the job.

Not because of the dead.

Because of the waiting.

Every night, he climbed the stone tower and sat beside the bell with a lantern, listening to the wind scrape against the windows.

Most nights were quiet.

Some nights weren't.

He had heard the bell ring twice more times than he could remember.

He had never heard it ring three times.

Until tonight.

Dong.

Elias froze.

The sound rolled across the town.

Once.

He slowly stood.

His hand tightened around the lantern.

Dong.

Twice.

Below him, windows opened.

People looked outside.

Nobody spoke.

Elias stared at the bell.

Then came the third sound.

Dong.

The entire town went silent.

Somewhere below, a child started crying.

Elias didn't move.

He knew the rule.

When the bell rang three times, you didn't investigate.

You didn't look for the body.

You didn't ask who had died.

You left.

But then he heard something else.

A voice.

Coming from inside the tower.

“Elias.”

His blood went cold.

He knew that voice.

It belonged to his brother.

His brother had been dead for eleven years.

Elias ran downstairs.

The streets were already empty.

Doors slammed shut as he passed.

People had abandoned everything.

A basket of bread.

A child's wooden toy.

A horse still tied outside a shop.

The whole town was running toward the eastern gate.

Elias ran in the opposite direction.

Toward the old cemetery.

He didn't know why.

Maybe because his brother's voice was coming from there.

Maybe because some stupid part of him wanted to hear it again.

The cemetery gates were open.

They were never supposed to be open.

He stepped inside.

The graves were empty.

Every single one.

Not disturbed.

Not broken.

Just empty.

Elias raised his lantern.

Hundreds of open graves stretched across the field.

Then he saw the footprints.

Bare feet.

Too many.

All of them leading toward the forest.

He followed.

The forest had been dead for as long as Elias could remember.

No birds.

No insects.

No wind.

The trees stood completely still, their branches twisted together like fingers.

He walked for nearly an hour.

Then he found the village.

It wasn't on any map.

There were houses.

A well.

A church.

People walking through the streets.

Elias stared.

Every person he saw was dead.

He recognized some of them.

The baker.

The old blacksmith.

A woman who had died during winter.

Children.

Dozens of them.

Hundreds.

All walking quietly.

All heading toward the church.

Elias stepped backward.

Someone grabbed his shoulder.

He nearly screamed.

An old man stood behind him.

His face was gray.

His eyes were completely black.

“You shouldn't be here.”

Elias couldn't speak.

The old man pointed toward the church.

“He's waiting.”

“Who?”

The old man smiled.

“The King beneath the earth.”

Inside the church, there were no candles.

Only bones.

Thousands of them.

Stacked along the walls.

At the end of the room sat a man on a throne.

He looked young.

Maybe twenty.

Maybe a thousand.

A crown of black iron rested on his head.

Elias knew him immediately.

Not because he had seen him before.

Because everyone in Veyr had heard the story.

The first king of Veyr.

The man who supposedly disappeared before the kingdom existed.

The man who had made the first bell.

Elias stared at him.

“You're dead.”

The king laughed.

“So are you.”

Elias looked down.

His hands were transparent.

His lantern fell through his fingers.

“No.”

The king stood.

“Your town burned you six years ago.”

Elias shook his head.

“I've been the Bellkeeper for six years.”

“Yes.”

“People spoke to me.”

“Yes.”

“I ate.”

“Yes.”

“I slept.”

The king smiled.

“You remember what you ate?”

Elias went silent.

“Do you remember your mother's face?”

Nothing.

“Your brother?”

Elias felt something crack inside him.

“Stop.”

“Your own death?”

He couldn't breathe.

The king walked closer.

“You were the first body to rise when the bell rang three times.”

Elias looked toward the doorway.

The dead villagers were watching him.

“They've been waiting for you.”

“Why?”

The king pointed toward the ceiling.

Far above them, beyond the forest, beyond the abandoned cemetery...

the bell began to ring.

Dong.

Elias stared upward.

Dong.

The dead began kneeling.

Dong.

The king lowered his head.

And for the first time, Elias understood.

The bell had never been warning the living.

It had been calling the dead.

The king removed his crown.

Then he placed it in Elias's hands.

“I've been ringing the bell for a thousand years.”

Elias stared at the crown.

“What happens if I don't take it?”

The king smiled sadly.

“Then someone else will.”

“And if I do?”

“You become the thing that keeps this kingdom alive.”

Elias looked toward the distant town.

He could almost see it.

The people running.

The locked doors.

The frightened children.

People who still believed the dead were something to fear.

He looked back at the king.

“Why me?”

The king's expression changed.

“Because you were the only one who came looking.”

Elias looked at the crown.

It was warm.

Almost like it had a heartbeat.

He put it on.

The old king disappeared.

The church went dark.

And somewhere far away, every grave in Veyr opened at once.

The next morning, the people of Veyr returned.

There was no Bellkeeper in the tower.

Only a lantern.

And beside it, a crown made of black iron.

Nobody touched it.

Nobody knew what had happened.

Years passed.

The kingdom rebuilt itself.

Children grew up.

Wars were fought.

Kings died.

And eventually, people stopped believing in the old stories.

They said the dead were harmless.

They said the bells were only bells.

They said monsters didn't exist.

Then, one winter night, a boy climbing the tower heard a sound behind him.

A man stood in the darkness.

Black crown.

Pale face.

Eyes like empty wells.

The boy dropped his candle.

“Who are you?”

The man looked toward the sleeping kingdom.

Then he pulled the rope.

Dong.

The boy screamed.

The man smiled.

“I'm the Bellkeeper.”

And beneath the earth...

something answered.

Veyr
