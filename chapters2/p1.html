Prequel — The Petal That Falls With a Smile

The First Snow

Chapter 1  POV: Sora

The first snow always made the town quiet.

Not completely quiet.

You could still hear cars passing through the main road, the distant sound of a train, and children shouting somewhere behind the houses.

But everything felt softer.

The rooftops were white.

The streets were white.

Even the mountains in the distance looked like someone had erased their edges with a pencil.

I pressed my hand against the cold window.

“Snow…”

I smiled.

I had been waiting for it all week.

“Sora!”

I turned around.

Ren stood in the doorway, wearing a thick winter jacket that was clearly too big for him.

“What?” I asked.

“You said you'd come outside.”

“I know.”

“You've been staring at the snow for ten minutes.”

“I was appreciating it.”

Ren stared at me.

“You're weird.”

I frowned.

“I'm not.”

“You are.”

I grabbed a pillow.

He immediately stepped backward.

“Don't.”

“You called me weird.”

“I take it back.”

“Too late.”

I threw the pillow.

It hit him directly in the face.

He stood there silently.

Then he picked it up.

“You're going to regret that.”

I laughed and ran.

“Sora!”

I rushed outside before he could catch me.

The cold air hit my face immediately.

“Ah!”

I pulled my scarf higher.

Ren came running after me.

“You didn't even put your gloves on!”

“I forgot!”

“You always forget.”

“And you always complain.”

“Because you always forget!”

I stuck my tongue out at him.

He sighed.

Then he smiled.

We walked toward the small hill behind the neighborhood.

It was where everyone went whenever it snowed.

Children were already there, dragging sleds and throwing snowballs.

Ren picked up a handful of snow.

I immediately became suspicious.

“Ren.”

“What?”

“Don't.”

“I haven't done anything.”

“You're thinking about it.”

He smiled.

That was enough.

I ran.

A snowball flew past my shoulder.

“REN!”

He laughed.

And I chased him through the snow.

For a while, there was nothing else.

No school.

No transfers.

No goodbyes.

No future.

Just two middle-school kids running through the snow in a small northern town.

Eventually, we collapsed beneath an old tree at the edge of the hill.

I looked up.

Its branches were completely bare.

“Ren.”

“Hmm?”

“Do you think it'll bloom again?”

He looked at the tree.

“Of course.”

“Even after winter?”

“Yeah.”

“How do you know?”

He shrugged.

“Because trees don't just give up.”

I smiled.

“That sounds like something an old man would say.”

“Shut up.”

I laughed again.

A tiny snowflake landed on my hand.

Then another.

I watched them disappear.

“Ren?”

“Yeah?”

“Promise me something.”

He turned toward me.

“What?”

I looked at the tree.

“When we're older…”

I paused.

“…let's come back here.”

Ren smiled.

“Okay.”

“And if we forget?”

“I won't.”

“You don't know that.”

“I do.”

“How?”

He looked at me with the most serious expression I'd ever seen on his face.

“Because I'll remind you.”

I smiled.

“Promise?”

“Promise.”

We bumped our fists together.

Neither of us knew how important that promise would become.

At the time—

It was just another winter.

Just another day.

Just two kids making a promise they were certain they'd never forget.

End of Chapter 1
