Ch. 06

April 17

the fourth person.

I didn't throw the notebook away.

I thought about it all weekend.

Saturday morning, I put it on my desk.

Then I put it in a drawer.

Then I took it out again.

At lunch, I put it under my bed.

At around four, I took it back.

This was becoming stupid.

It was a notebook.

A notebook shouldn't be able to ruin an entire weekend.

Mine apparently could.

I didn't open it on Saturday.

I didn't open it on Sunday either.

That was a lie.

I opened it Sunday night.

The page still said:

You were there too.

Underneath it was the date.

April 17.

I searched my phone calendar.

April 17.

Last year.

Nothing.

I checked my photos.

I scrolled past screenshots, food, random pictures of my desk, Kenta making an ugly face during lunch...

Then I stopped.

There was a photo from April 17.

I didn't remember taking it.

It was a picture of the classroom.

The same classroom.

The same windows.

The same desks.

It looked completely ordinary.

Except for one thing.

There were four people in the picture.

I zoomed in.

Me.

Haru.

Kenta.

And someone sitting by the window.

The fourth person's face was covered by the reflection of the glass.

I stared at it for a long time.

Then I checked the information.

The photo had been taken at 4:26 p.m.

I looked at the picture again.

School usually ended at four.

Why were we still there?

I had no idea.

I checked my messages from that day.

Most of them were gone.

Not deleted.

Just... gone.

There was a gap.

4:03 p.m.

Then the next message was at 7:18 p.m.

Three hours missing.

I sat back in my chair.

"Okay."

I said it out loud because I didn't know what else to say.

The next morning, I went to school early again.

I wanted to ask Haru about the photo.

He wasn't there.

Kenta arrived five minutes later.

He dropped his bag onto his desk.

"You look horrible."

"Good morning to you too."

"Did you sleep?"

"Not much."

"Again?"

"Yeah."

He sat down.

I pulled out my phone.

"Do you remember April 17 last year?"

He looked at me.

"Why?"

"Just answer."

He thought about it.

"No."

"You don't?"

"Should I?"

"I don't know."

"Wasn't that when Ren transferred?"

"Maybe."

Kenta frowned.

"Why are you asking?"

I showed him the photo.

He took my phone.

His expression changed.

"Who's that?"

"I don't know."

He zoomed in.

"That's weird."

"Yeah."

"Is that you?"

"Yes."

"And Haru."

"Yeah."

"Then who's the fourth person?"

"I think it's Ren."

Kenta stopped.

He looked at me.

"Why do you think that?"

"The notebook."

He sighed.

"You really haven't stopped with that thing."

"No."

He handed my phone back.

"I don't remember being there."

"Neither do I."

"Maybe we weren't."

"But the photo..."

"Photos can be edited."

I stared at him.

"Do you think I'm editing old school photos?"

"No."

"Then?"

"I don't know."

He looked uncomfortable.

That was new.

Kenta usually had an answer for everything, even if it was completely wrong.

I lowered my voice.

"Do you remember Ren?"

He scratched his cheek.

"Kind of."

"Kind of?"

"I remember someone."

"That's not the same thing."

"I know."

He looked toward the window.

"I remember sitting with him once."

"When?"

"I don't know."

"Last year?"

"Maybe."

"What did you talk about?"

Kenta frowned.

"I can't remember."

"Anything?"

"No."

He paused.

"Actually..."

"What?"

"He asked me if I'd ever forgotten something important."

I stared at him.

"What did you say?"

"I probably made a joke."

"You don't remember?"

"No."

I looked at the clock.

The classroom was filling up.

I wanted to ask more.

But Kenta suddenly said,

"Don't tell Haru I told you."

"Why?"

"Because he doesn't like talking about Ren."

"Why?"

"I just said he doesn't."

Before I could answer, our teacher walked in.

The day started.

I tried to concentrate.

I failed.

During second period, my phone vibrated.

Unknown number.

One message.

Look under the third desk.

I stared at it.

I looked around the classroom.

Nobody seemed to be watching me.

The third desk was behind me.

I waited until the teacher turned toward the board.

Then I crouched down.

There was nothing underneath.

I checked the sides.

Nothing.

I was about to sit back up when my fingers touched something taped beneath the desk.

A small envelope.

I pulled it free.

My name was written on the front.

Not my full name.

Just:

Aki.

I froze.

I didn't recognize the handwriting.

I opened it beneath the desk.

Inside was a photograph.

Another picture from last year.

This one was taken inside the classroom.

The date was written on the back.

April 17.

There were five people in it.

Me.

Haru.

Kenta.

Ren.

And someone else.

A girl.

I stared at her.

I knew her.

I just couldn't remember from where.

She was standing near the door, holding a bag.

Her face was turned toward the camera.

Unlike Ren, her face wasn't blurred.

I knew that face.

I had seen it recently.

I flipped the photograph over.

There was one sentence written underneath the date.

Ask her what happened after this picture.

My hands went cold.

I looked toward the door.

Nobody was standing there.

Then I looked at the classroom.

The girl in the photograph was no longer a mystery.

She was sitting two rows ahead of me.

Mina.

I stared at the back of her head.

She suddenly turned around.

Our eyes met.

She smiled.

"Why are you staring at me?"

I couldn't answer.

She tilted her head.

"Are you okay?"

I looked down at the photograph.

Then back at her.

"Mina..."

"Yeah?"

"Where were you on April 17 last year?"

Her smile disappeared.

Not slowly.

Immediately.

She looked at the photograph in my hand.

Then at me.

For several seconds, neither of us spoke.

Finally, she whispered,

"Where did you get that?"

I didn't answer.

She stood up.

"Give it to me."

"No."

"Aki."

"I just want to know what happened."

She looked toward the teacher.

Then back at me.

Her voice became quieter.

"You really shouldn't have found that."

"What happened?"

She didn't answer.

Instead, she sat down.

For the rest of the lesson, she didn't look at me once.

When the bell rang, she left immediately.

I followed.

"Mina!"

She kept walking.

I caught up with her near the stairs.

"Wait."

She stopped.

I held up the photograph.

"Please."

She looked at it.

Then closed her eyes.

"When did you start remembering?"

I frowned.

"What?"

She opened her eyes.

"You don't remember, do you?"

"Remember what?"

She laughed once.

It wasn't a happy laugh.

More like she couldn't believe what she was hearing.

"Of course."

"What?"

She looked around the hallway.

Then lowered her voice.

"Ren didn't disappear."

I stared at her.

"What do you mean?"

"He was there the next day."

"At school?"

"No."

She looked directly at me.

"At your house."

I couldn't speak.

She walked away.

I stood on the stairs, completely still.

Then my phone vibrated.

Another message.

This time, there was no number.

Just a sentence.

Ask yourself why you don't remember going home that day.
