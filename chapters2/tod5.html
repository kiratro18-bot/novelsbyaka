Ch. 05

Friday

you were there too.

I spent the entire night trying to remember a face I couldn't see.

It was annoying.

The harder I tried, the worse it got.

I remembered a voice.

Sort of.

A laugh.

Maybe.

A school uniform.

Definitely.

But whenever I tried to put a face to it, my brain seemed to replace it with a blank space.

At some point, I gave up.

I turned over in bed and checked my phone.

1:18 a.m.

I had school in the morning.

"Great."

I put the phone down.

Then picked it up again.

I searched for Shinoda Ren.

Nothing useful.

Just a couple of unrelated results.

I searched the school name.

Still nothing.

I almost searched the name in our class group chat.

Then I stopped.

If someone asked why I was searching for him, I'd have to explain.

I didn't want to explain.

Especially not yet.

I finally fell asleep.

When I woke up, I had forgotten about the whole thing for approximately twelve seconds.

Then I saw the black notebook on my desk.

Right.

Friday.

I opened it.

The page was still there.

Someone will ask what happened to Ren.

I stared at it.

"Okay."

I closed it.

There was no point guessing anymore.

I was just going to see what happened.

At school, Kenta was already complaining.

"Why is Friday so far from Monday?"

"It's the same distance every week."

"Exactly. That's the problem."

"You make no sense."

"I make perfect sense."

I put my bag down.

Mina walked past us.

"He's been saying that since first period."

"See?" Kenta said. "Even she understands."

"I didn't say that."

"Close enough."

I smiled.

For a few minutes, everything felt normal again.

Then our homeroom teacher walked in.

She put her books down.

"Before we begin, I need to discuss something."

Nobody paid much attention.

She looked toward the back of the room.

"Some of you may remember a student named Shinoda Ren."

The room changed.

Not dramatically.

Nobody gasped.

Nobody stood up.

It was smaller than that.

A few people stopped talking.

Kenta looked at me.

Haru looked down at his desk.

Our teacher continued.

"He attended this class last year."

I felt my hands tighten around my pencil.

"He transferred schools during the second term."

Someone raised a hand.

"Where did he transfer?"

The teacher paused.

"I don't have that information."

That answer seemed to satisfy everyone.

Except me.

I looked at Haru.

He wasn't looking at me.

Our teacher continued with the lesson.

I didn't hear much after that.

The notebook had predicted it.

Someone would ask what happened to Ren.

But it wasn't me.

It was the teacher.

I opened the notebook beneath my desk.

The Friday page hadn't changed.

That was when I noticed something.

There was writing underneath the prediction.

Very faint.

Almost impossible to see.

I tilted the notebook toward the light.

Three words.

Not what happened.

I frowned.

I rubbed the page.

Nothing.

I looked at the next line.

Blank.

I flipped forward.

There was another page.

No date.

Ask what happened on Friday.

I stared at it.

Then I looked at the clock.

It was 9:42.

Friday had barely started.

I closed the notebook.

I decided I wasn't going to do anything stupid.

That lasted until lunch.

Kenta was eating noodles.

I was eating bread.

Mina was complaining about the bread in the cafeteria.

"Why does it taste like cardboard?"

"You keep buying it."

"I keep hoping they'll improve."

"They haven't."

"I admire persistence."

Kenta laughed.

I didn't.

I was looking at Haru.

He was sitting alone near the window.

I stood up.

"Where are you going?" Kenta asked.

"Bathroom."

"You just went."

"I forgot something."

"In the bathroom?"

"Yeah."

He stared at me.

"You're weird."

"I know."

I walked toward Haru.

He looked up.

"What?"

"I want to ask you something."

"No."

"I haven't asked yet."

"I know what you're going to ask."

I sat down opposite him.

"What happened on Friday?"

His expression changed.

"Which Friday?"

"Last year."

He looked away.

"I don't remember."

"You do."

"No."

"Haru."

"I said I don't."

I lowered my voice.

"The notebook told me to ask."

He went completely still.

For a second, I thought he might leave.

Instead, he asked,

"What did it say?"

I showed him the page.

He read it.

His face went pale.

Not dramatically.

He just stopped moving.

Then he took the notebook from my hands.

He flipped through the pages.

Tuesday.

Wednesday.

Thursday.

Friday.

He stopped at the page with the faint writing.

His thumb moved across it.

"Where did you find this?"

"Under my desk."

"No."

"What?"

"Where did you really find it?"

"I told you."

He looked at me.

"That's not where I found it."

I didn't understand.

"What do you mean?"

Haru closed the notebook.

"I found it too."

"When?"

"Last year."

My stomach dropped.

"You had it?"

"Yeah."

"Then why did you say you didn't?"

"Because I didn't want to talk about it."

"About the notebook?"

"About Ren."

I waited.

Haru looked toward the window.

"Ren was obsessed with it."

"Why?"

"I don't know."

"Where did he get it?"

"I don't know."

"Then what happened?"

He rubbed his forehead.

"Every day, he'd write something down."

"Predictions?"

"Sometimes."

"Sometimes?"

"Other things."

"Like what?"

Haru didn't answer.

"Like what?"

He finally looked at me.

"Things that had already happened."

I frowned.

"What's the difference?"

"The dates."

"What?"

"He'd write something that happened months ago."

"How would he know?"

"He wouldn't."

I stared at him.

Haru continued quietly.

"That's why he started getting scared."

I didn't know what to say.

"Scared of the notebook?"

"No."

He looked at me.

"Scared that he was remembering things that never happened."

The cafeteria noise seemed to fade for a moment.

I looked down at the notebook.

"Did Ren disappear because of it?"

"I don't know."

"You said he stopped coming to school."

"Yeah."

"Then what?"

"Then nothing."

"That's not an answer."

"It's the only one I have."

I wanted to keep asking.

But something about his face stopped me.

He looked exhausted.

Like he'd answered these questions before.

Maybe too many times.

I stood up.

Haru grabbed my wrist.

Not hard.

Just enough to stop me.

"Don't open tomorrow's page."

I looked at him.

"Why?"

"Because tomorrow isn't written for you."

He let go.

I laughed nervously.

"That's a weird thing to say."

"I know."

I walked back to my seat.

Kenta looked at me.

"What did he want?"

"Nothing."

"You're saying that a lot lately."

"Yeah."

After school, I stayed behind.

I waited until everyone had left.

Then I opened the notebook.

I turned to the next page.

There was no date.

Just one sentence.

Saturday.

I stared at it.

Then another line appeared underneath.

Not written in fresh ink.

It had been there all along.

I was sure of it.

You were there too.

I stopped breathing for a second.

I read it again.

Then again.

My phone buzzed.

A message from an unknown number.

Don't ask Haru about Ren again.

I stared at the screen.

Another message arrived.

He doesn't remember everything.

I looked toward the classroom door.

Nobody was there.

I typed:

Who is this?

The message showed as delivered.

No reply.

I waited.

Nothing.

I looked at the notebook again.

For the first time, I noticed something on the bottom corner of the page.

A small handwritten date.

April 17.

I didn't recognize it.

Then I realized something.

It wasn't this year's April 17.

It was last year's.

The day Ren disappeared.

I closed the notebook.

And for the first time since finding it, I seriously considered throwing it away.

I didn't.

I put it back in my bag.

Then I went home.

I still didn't know why.

Maybe I was curious.

Maybe I was stupid.

Probably both.
