Ch. 01

The Notebook

found beneath a desk. no name on the cover.

I was late to school because I couldn't find my left sock.

Not both socks.

Just the left one.

I found the right one under my bed, which somehow made things worse because now I knew the other one had to be somewhere stupid.

It was eventually found hanging from the handle of my desk drawer.

I still don't know how it got there.

By the time I reached the classroom, the first bell had already rung.

I stood outside for a second, trying to catch my breath without making it obvious that I was trying to catch my breath.

Our classroom door was open.

Mr. Sakamoto was writing something on the board.

He looked at me.

I looked at him.

He looked at the clock.

Then back at me.

"Good morning."

"Good morning, sir."

"You're late."

"I know."

"Wonderful."

I wasn't sure if that was sarcasm.

Probably.

I walked to my seat while everyone else pretended not to look at me.

Someone whispered, "Nice of you to join us."

I turned around.

It was Kenta.

"I almost died."

"Because you were late?"

"Because I ran."

"You live ten minutes away."

"I ran emotionally."

He stared at me.

"That doesn't mean anything."

"I know."

He laughed quietly.

Mr. Sakamoto cleared his throat.

We both turned around.

"Would you like to continue?"

"No, sir."

"Good."

I sat down.

The morning went on.

Math.

History.

A short break.

Then English.

Nothing happened.

At least, nothing worth remembering.

That's what I thought at the time.

During lunch, Kenta stole two pieces of my omelet.

I told him he was a criminal.

He said criminals didn't ask permission.

Then he stole another one.

I stopped sharing.

Across the room, Mina was arguing with Ayaka about whether a convenience-store pudding counted as lunch.

"It has eggs in it," Mina said.

"That's not how nutrition works."

"It has milk too."

"That's dessert."

"You're just jealous."

"I don't want pudding."

"You look jealous."

"I literally don't."

They kept arguing.

I ate the last piece of my omelet.

The classroom was noisy in the comfortable way it usually was during lunch. Chairs scraping against the floor. Someone laughing too loudly. A phone playing music from somewhere near the windows.

I liked that noise.

It made school feel less like school.

After lunch, we had chemistry.

Mr. Sakamoto wasn't our chemistry teacher, but apparently he was covering the class because our actual teacher was absent.

He walked in carrying a stack of papers.

"Before we begin, I need someone to collect these."

Everyone suddenly became extremely interested in their desks.

I looked down too.

Kenta whispered, "Don't make eye contact."

"I know."

"He's hunting."

"I know."

"He's getting closer."

"Shut up."

"Aki."

I looked up.

Mr. Sakamoto was standing beside my desk.

"Could you take these to the staff room after class?"

I sighed.

"Sure."

"Thank you."

He placed the papers on my desk.

Kenta leaned over.

"Congratulations."

"On what?"

"Community service."

I ignored him.

The class ended forty minutes later.

I carried the papers toward the staff room.

On the way, I remembered I had forgotten my notebook.

So I went back.

The classroom was empty.

Almost.

One of the desks near the back was still pulled out.

I walked over.

It belonged to Haru.

Haru had been absent for three days.

Nobody seemed particularly worried about it.

At least, nobody had said anything.

I picked up the notebook lying beside his desk.

I was going to put it inside his bag.

Then I noticed something underneath the desk.

A black notebook.

Not a school notebook.

Just a plain one.

I crouched down and pulled it out.

There was no name on it.

I looked toward the door.

Nobody was there.

I probably should have left it.

Instead, I opened it.

The first page was blank.

So was the second.

The third had a date written at the top.

Tuesday.

Someone will drop their phone during lunch.

That was today.

Underneath it was a single sentence.

I stared at it.

Then laughed.

"Okay."

It was probably some stupid joke.

I flipped the page.

Nothing.

Another page.

Nothing.

I turned back.

Tuesday.

Someone will drop their phone during lunch.

I remembered Mina dropping her phone that afternoon.

It had slipped from her hand when she stood up.

She'd complained about it for five minutes.

I hadn't thought much about it.

Until now.

I looked at the sentence again.

Maybe it had been written after it happened.

Obviously.

That was the explanation.

I checked the date.

No year.

Just Tuesday.

I closed the notebook.

Then opened it again.

For some reason, I checked the next page.

There was another sentence.

Wednesday.

Someone will forget their umbrella.

I looked outside.

The sky was clear.

No rain.

I frowned.

Then I shrugged.

Coincidence.

Probably.

I closed the notebook and shoved it into my bag.

I told myself I'd return it tomorrow.

I didn't know whose it was.

But someone would probably ask for it.

I picked up my chemistry notebook and left.

By the time I got home, I had completely forgotten about it.

At least for a few hours.

That night, it rained.

Not heavily.

Just enough to make the streets shiny.

I was standing near the convenience store when I remembered the notebook.

I stopped.

"Ah."

I looked toward the school in the distance.

Then at the rain.

Wednesday.

Someone will forget their umbrella.

I laughed.

"That's ridiculous."

I had no idea why I found it funny.

Maybe because it suddenly didn't feel like a coincidence anymore.

I stood there for a while.

Then I went home.

The next morning, I brought the notebook with me.

I wanted to see what would happen.

I didn't tell Kenta.

I didn't tell anyone.

I don't really know why.

Maybe I thought they'd make fun of me.

Maybe I wanted to be the only person who knew.

Or maybe I was just curious.

At 8:17 a.m., I saw Haru standing outside the classroom.

He looked at me.

I looked at him.

He smiled.

"Morning."

I blinked.

"You're back."

"Yeah."

He walked past me and sat at his desk.

I stood there for a second.

Three days absent.

No explanation.

Nobody asked.

Then the bell rang.

And everyone went back to their ordinary morning.

I reached into my bag.

The notebook was still there.

I opened it beneath my desk.

The page for Wednesday was unchanged.

Wednesday.

Someone will forget their umbrella.

I looked outside.

The rain had stopped.

I smiled.

"Guess it was nothing."

I closed the notebook.

I was wrong.

But I wouldn't find that out until later.

Much later.

For now, it was just another Wednesday.

— end of chapter one —
