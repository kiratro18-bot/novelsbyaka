Ch. 03

The Question

some names disappear quietly.

The next morning, I arrived at school earlier than usual.

Not because I wanted to.

My mother had left for work early and forgotten to wake me up, which she later claimed was my fault somehow.

"You have an alarm."

"I slept through it."

"Then set two."

"That's not how waking up works."

"Apparently it is."

So I left home twenty minutes earlier than usual.

Which meant I got to school before most people.

The classroom was almost empty.

Haru was already there.

Of course he was.

He was sitting at his desk, looking out the window.

I stopped at the door.

He looked over.

"You're early."

"So are you."

"I've been here."

"I can see that."

"Good."

I walked to my seat.

For some reason, I felt nervous.

The notebook was inside my bag.

I hadn't opened it yet.

I didn't want to.

Which was stupid.

It was just a notebook.

Probably someone's stupid notebook.

Probably.

I sat down and took out my textbooks.

Haru looked at me.

"Can I ask you something?"

My stomach dropped.

I looked at him.

"Yeah?"

He pointed at my bag.

"What's that?"

I followed his finger.

The notebook wasn't visible.

"What's what?"

"The black thing."

I looked again.

A corner of the notebook was sticking out of my bag.

I quickly pushed it inside.

"Nothing."

Haru stared at me.

"That's a pretty suspicious answer."

"It's a normal answer."

"People don't say 'nothing' when something is nothing."

"You're doing too much thinking this early in the morning."

He laughed.

"Fair."

He turned back toward the window.

I let out a breath.

That was it.

That was the prediction.

Someone had asked me about the notebook.

It had happened exactly like the page said.

Except...

Haru hadn't asked what was inside.

He'd only asked what it was.

Still counted.

I think.

I reached into my bag.

I wanted to look at the page.

Just once.

But before I could take it out, Kenta came running into the classroom.

"Move!"

He nearly crashed into my desk.

I grabbed my pencil case before it fell.

"What is wrong with you?"

"Nothing."

"You almost killed my stationery."

"Your stationery will survive."

"My eraser is already emotionally damaged."

Kenta sat down.

Then he looked at Haru.

"Oh, you're here."

Haru nodded.

"You sound disappointed."

"I am."

They started arguing about something stupid.

I stopped listening.

My hand was still inside my bag.

I touched the notebook.

Then I pulled it out.

I opened it beneath my desk.

Thursday.

The sentence was still there.

Someone will ask me about this notebook.

I stared at it.

It had happened.

But something bothered me.

The handwriting.

I turned the page backward.

Tuesday.

Someone will drop their phone during lunch.

Wednesday.

Someone will forget their umbrella.

Thursday.

Someone will ask me about this notebook.

The handwriting looked almost identical.

Except the last sentence.

The letters were slightly smaller.

I wasn't sure if that meant anything.

Probably not.

I closed the notebook.

Then I noticed something written faintly at the bottom of the page.

I hadn't seen it before.

A few words.

Almost erased.

I leaned closer.

Don't show them.

I froze.

Someone behind me tapped my shoulder.

I nearly jumped out of my chair.

"Whoa."

I turned around.

Mina was standing there.

"You okay?"

"Yeah."

"You look like you saw a ghost."

"I didn't."

She pointed at my hand.

"What's that?"

I closed the notebook.

"Nothing."

She raised an eyebrow.

"Again with the nothing."

I shoved it into my bag.

"It's just an old notebook."

"Yours?"

"No."

"Then whose?"

"I don't know."

"Then why do you have it?"

I didn't answer.

She waited.

I could feel Kenta looking at me too.

Finally, I said, "I found it."

"Where?"

"School."

"Where in school?"

"Under a desk."

"Whose desk?"

I hesitated.

"Haru."

The room suddenly felt quieter.

Haru looked up.

"Mine?"

"Yeah."

He frowned.

"I don't have a notebook like that."

"You don't?"

"No."

Mina looked at me.

Then at Haru.

Then back at me.

"That's weird."

"Yeah."

Haru leaned back in his chair.

"When did you find it?"

"Yesterday."

"Why didn't you give it to me?"

"I thought it was yours."

He stared at me for a second.

Then smiled.

"Fair enough."

But something about his expression bothered me.

Not because he looked suspicious.

He didn't.

If anything, he looked confused.

Genuinely confused.

The bell rang before anyone could say anything else.

Mr. Sakamoto walked in.

Everyone returned to their seats.

I opened my textbook.

For the next forty minutes, I tried to concentrate.

I failed.

During the lesson, I kept thinking about one thing.

Haru had been absent for three days.

The notebook had been under his desk.

He said it wasn't his.

So whose was it?

After class, I went to the library.

I wasn't looking for anything specific.

At least, that's what I told myself.

I checked the old school records.

Most of them were boring.

Names.

Classes.

Attendance.

Nothing interesting.

Then I found something.

A class list from last year.

I scanned the names.

There was Haru.

Kenta.

Mina.

Me.

Then another name.

I stopped.

I had never seen it before.

Shinoda Ren.

I stared at it.

There was a small note beside the name.

Transferred.

That was all.

I turned the page.

Nothing.

I searched the next few records.

Ren's name appeared again.

Then suddenly—

nothing.

No transfer record.

No new school.

No explanation.

Just a name that disappeared.

I closed the file.

Maybe it meant nothing.

People transferred all the time.

I put the folder back.

As I walked out of the library, I saw Haru standing at the end of the hallway.

He was looking at me.

I stopped.

He smiled.

"Looking for something?"

I didn't answer immediately.

Then I shook my head.

"No."

He nodded.

"Okay."

He walked past me.

After a few steps, he stopped.

Without turning around, he said,

"By the way..."

I looked at him.

"Don't worry about the notebook."

My heart skipped.

I hadn't told him what was written inside.

Haru turned around.

For the first time since I'd met him, he wasn't smiling.

"I don't think you should read it anymore."

Then he walked away.

I stood there alone in the hallway.

I reached into my bag.

The notebook was still there.

I opened it.

The Thursday page was unchanged.

Except for one thing.

The faint words at the bottom were no longer there.

Don't show them.

They had disappeared.

I stared at the empty space.

Then I turned the page.

There was another sentence.

One I hadn't seen before.

No date.

No explanation.

Just four words.

You already know why.
